Why Surrey Demolition Permits Stall

WHAT TO HAVE READY BEFORE YOU APPLY

When a demolition gets delayed, the permit is usually not what people were waiting on. They were waiting on a document the permit application asked for — and that document had its own lead time nobody accounted for.

The pattern repeats on almost every teardown we get called into late. Someone submits the application, discovers a required attachment is missing, and then finds out the missing attachment takes two to three weeks to produce. The clock was never running on the City. It was running on the applicant.

Here is what a demolition application generally asks you to have in hand, and which items carry real lead time.

The hazardous materials report is the long pole

Before a building in British Columbia is demolished or substantially renovated, a qualified person must inspect it for hazardous materials. This is a WorkSafeBC requirement under the Occupational Health and Safety Regulation, and it applies regardless of the building's age or condition. Municipalities generally want to see that report — or confirmation it exists — as part of a demolition permit application.

That report is not something you can produce on a Friday afternoon. The realistic sequence is:

  • Scheduling the survey — a qualified consultant has to attend the site, and good ones are booked out.
  • Destructive sampling — samples come out of walls, ceilings, flooring layers, and mechanical insulation. This means access to the building, and sometimes power.
  • Lab turnaround — standard analysis typically runs several business days. Rush options exist and cost more.
  • Report production — findings written up into the document the City and your contractors will work from.

Start to finish, budget two to three weeks unless you are paying for rush at every stage. If the survey finds asbestos, lead, or other regulated materials, the abatement scope that follows adds its own timeline on top of this — see what happens after a positive result.

The common mistake

Ordering the survey after the offer closes instead of during due diligence. On a property you already expect to demolish, the survey can often be arranged before you own the building, with the seller's cooperation. That single change can pull two weeks out of the front of your schedule.

Utility disconnections are scheduled by other people

Gas, electrical, and water services need to be disconnected and confirmed before demolition proceeds, and each one is booked through a different organization on their own calendar. These are not same-week items, and in most cases they cannot be compressed by paying more.

The failure mode here is sequential booking — calling the gas utility, waiting for that to complete, then calling the next one. Book them in parallel the day the purchase closes. The confirmations you receive are what the City and your contractors will want to see.

Deposits, fees, and site protection

Demolition applications typically involve a security deposit held against site restoration and adjacent infrastructure, plus requirements around tree protection, sediment and erosion control, and site fencing. None of these has long lead time on its own, but each is a reason an otherwise complete application gets returned.

Fee amounts, deposit values, and specific submission requirements change. Confirm current figures directly with the City of Surrey before building them into a pro forma — do not rely on what a previous project cost.

A realistic order of operations

  1. Arrange the hazardous materials survey — during due diligence if you can, immediately on closing if you cannot.
  2. Book all utility disconnections in parallel on day one. Do not sequence them.
  3. Review the survey findings and scope any required abatement, including the licensed contractor who will perform it.
  4. Assemble the permit package with the report, disconnection confirmations, and site protection plan attached.
  5. Submit, and schedule abatement and demolition against the permit rather than in hope of it.

Run this way, a straightforward single-lot teardown is a manageable sequence. Run in the other order — permit first, discover requirements second — it becomes the six-week delay everyone downstream inherits.
